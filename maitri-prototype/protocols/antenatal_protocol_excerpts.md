# Antenatal & Postnatal Protocol Excerpts — MAITRI Offline Co-Pilot

> **Note on this file:** These are plain-language summaries of real Government of India
> maternal health programmes and guidelines, written for grounding the MAITRI assistant —
> not verbatim reproductions of the source documents. Before using this in anything beyond
> a prototype demo, have a clinician cross-check each excerpt against the current official
> document (linked under each source) in case guidance has been revised.

## ANC-01: Anaemia Detection and Management

**Source:** Anemia Mukt Bharat – Operational Guidelines, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoHFW) — nhm.gov.in

Pregnant women are screened for anaemia at every antenatal contact using a haemoglobin
test. A woman is classified as anaemic if her Hb falls below 11 g/dL in the first and
third trimester, or below 10.5 g/dL in the second trimester; severe anaemia is Hb below
7 g/dL. Every pregnant woman, regardless of Hb result, is given one iron-folic acid (IFA)
tablet daily from the fourth month of pregnancy (start of the second trimester), continued
for a minimum of 180 days through pregnancy and a further 180 days postpartum, plus a
single dose of albendazole for deworming after the first trimester. A woman confirmed
anaemic moves to a therapeutic dosing protocol under medical supervision; Hb below 7 g/dL
requires escalation for injectable iron, transfusion assessment, or referral to a higher
facility rather than continuing on the standard prophylactic dose.

## ANC-02: Hypertension and Pre-eclampsia

**Source:** National Guidelines for Calcium Supplementation During Pregnancy and Lactation, MoHFW (2014); Anemia Mukt Bharat protocol references

All pregnant women are counselled to take daily calcium supplementation (500 mg elemental
calcium with vitamin D, twice daily) from the fourth month of pregnancy through six months
postpartum, as a preventive measure against pre-eclampsia and eclampsia. Blood pressure is
checked at every contact: a systolic reading of 140 mmHg or higher, a diastolic reading of
90 mmHg or higher, or protein in the urine, is treated as pre-eclampsia and requires prompt
referral to a facility equipped for obstetric emergency care. Severe features — very high
BP, visual disturbance, epigastric pain, or convulsions — call for magnesium sulfate to
prevent eclamptic seizures and same-day transfer; this is never managed at sub-centre level.

## ANC-03: Referral Criteria for High-Risk Pregnancy

**Source:** Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA) / Extended PMSMA, MoHFW — mohfw.gov.in

The national programme maintains an expanded list of 25 high-risk pregnancy categories
used to flag and individually track women needing closer follow-up. Common triggers
include maternal age under 18 or over 35, height 140 cm or below, twin or multiple
pregnancy, severe anaemia, pre-eclampsia or eclampsia, a prior caesarean or obstetric
complication, bleeding during the current pregnancy, and gestational diabetes or another
significant medical comorbidity. A woman flagged under any HRP category is tagged to her
nearest First Referral Unit (FRU) and scheduled for three additional ASHA/ANM follow-up
visits beyond the routine schedule, with financial incentives supporting that extra contact.

## ANC-04: Antenatal Visit Schedule (FANC Protocol)

**Source:** Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan, MoHFW; national ANC guidelines

Every pregnant woman is expected to complete at least four antenatal contacts: the first
at registration (ideally within the first trimester), a second between roughly 14 and 26
weeks, a third between 28 and 34 weeks, and a fourth between 36 and 40 weeks — giving at
least one contact per trimester and two in the third trimester, when complications such as
pre-eclampsia most often emerge. Separately, PMSMA offers every pregnant woman in her
second or third trimester a fixed-day specialist antenatal check-up on the 9th of every
month at designated public facilities, and Extended PMSMA adds three further follow-up
visits specifically for women flagged as high risk.

## ANC-05: Danger Signs Requiring Immediate Action

**Source:** Guidebook for Enhancing Performance of Multi-Purpose Worker (Female), adapted for ASHA/ANM field use — vikaspedia.in

Any one of the following found during a home visit or antenatal contact is treated as an
immediate referral, not something to note and revisit at the next scheduled visit:
high fever; severe headache with blurred vision or dizziness; convulsions or fits;
swelling of the face, hands, or whole body; burning or painful urination, or increased
urination frequency; foul-smelling vaginal discharge with or without fever; high blood
pressure; watery vaginal discharge for more than 24 hours without labour pain; vaginal
bleeding; reduced or absent fetal movement; and palpitations or breathlessness at rest
(often a sign of severe anaemia).

## ANC-06: Nutritional Counselling and Supplementation

**Source:** National antenatal nutrition counselling guidance, MoHFW / Anemia Mukt Bharat

At every antenatal contact, weight and fundal height are tracked against expected growth
for gestational age. Poor or flat weight gain, or fundal height not increasing across two
consecutive visits, is treated as a possible sign of fetal growth restriction and prompts
closer follow-up rather than being noted for the next visit. Alongside IFA and calcium
supplementation, women are counselled on roughly 300–350 additional kcal per day from
locally available foods during pregnancy, with particular attention to correcting the
dietary gaps behind the very high anaemia burden seen in tribal antenatal populations.

## ANC-07: Foetal Growth Monitoring — Fundal Height

**Source:** National antenatal nutrition counselling guidance, MoHFW / Anemia Mukt Bharat

At every antenatal contact, weight and fundal height are tracked against expected growth
for gestational age. Poor or flat weight gain, or fundal height not increasing across two
consecutive visits, is treated as a possible sign of fetal growth restriction and prompts
closer follow-up rather than being noted for the next visit. Fundal height in centimetres
approximates gestational age in weeks after 20 weeks of pregnancy. Any flat or falling
fundal height measurement across two consecutive visits warrants urgent referral to assess
for intra-uterine growth restriction (IUGR).

## ANC-08: Postnatal Care Schedule (Day 1 to Day 42)

**Source:** MoHFW Home Based Postnatal Care guidelines (2013)

A mother who delivers in a facility receives six scheduled ASHA home visits after
delivery, on days 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, and 42. A mother who delivered at home receives a
seventh visit within the first 24 hours, in addition to the same six. Each visit covers
maternal recovery and danger-sign screening, breastfeeding support, newborn care, and
counselling on birth spacing and family planning, with prompt referral if any postpartum
complication is found.

During postnatal home visits, the newborn is also checked for: inability to feed or
breastfeed; repeated vomiting; convulsions; lethargy or unresponsiveness; fever or low
body temperature; fast or laboured breathing with chest indrawing; and significant skin
infection. Because roughly three-quarters of neonatal deaths occur within the first week
of life, any single danger sign found at a home visit is an immediate referral, not
something to hold until the next scheduled contact.

## ANC-09: Obstetric History and Risk Assessment

**Source:** Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA) / Extended PMSMA, MoHFW — mohfw.gov.in

The national programme maintains an expanded list of 25 high-risk pregnancy categories.
A woman is flagged as high-risk if she has: maternal age under 18 or over 35; height
140 cm or below; a prior caesarean section or obstetric complication (postpartum
haemorrhage, eclampsia, stillbirth, neonatal death); twin or multiple pregnancy; severe
anaemia (Hb < 7 g/dL); pre-eclampsia in the current pregnancy; bleeding at any point;
gestational diabetes; or any significant medical comorbidity. High-risk women are tagged
to their nearest First Referral Unit (FRU) and receive three additional ASHA/ANM follow-up
visits beyond the routine four, with financial incentives supporting that extra contact.

## ANC-10: Emergency Transport and Referral Process

**Source:** Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK), MoHFW, launched 2011

A pregnant woman referred from home to a public facility, or transferred between
facilities, is entitled to free transport both ways at no cost, along with free drugs,
diagnostics, blood, and diet during her stay, and a free drop-back home after delivery.
These entitlements exist specifically to remove the "she couldn't reach care" barrier — a
referral raised under JSSK is meant to come with an arranged vehicle, not only written
advice — and the same free-transport principle extends to sick newborns and infants up to
one year of age. The ANM initiates the referral by calling the 108 emergency ambulance
service, selecting the destination facility based on the level of care required (sub-district
hospital for moderate risk, FRU/district hospital for high-risk or obstetric emergency),
completing a referral slip, and communicating directly with the receiving facility before
the woman departs.

## ANC-11: Immunisation During Pregnancy

**Source:** National Immunization Schedule, MoHFW

A pregnant woman not already vaccinated in the current pregnancy receives the Td
(tetanus and adult diphtheria) vaccine at her antenatal visit, as two doses four weeks
apart, protecting both mother and newborn against tetanus. Td has replaced the standalone
tetanus toxoid (TT) vaccine as the current standard. Live vaccines (such as
rubella-containing vaccines) are contraindicated during pregnancy and are not part of
this schedule.
